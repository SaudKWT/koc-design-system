# Consumer report — v0.1.4 landing in the DWOS app

Answers to the four questions asked with the release. Measured in a running app,
both themes, as computed styles rather than screenshots.

**Short version: it lands, it reads right, and it broke one thing outside the
sidebar that is worth a design-system decision rather than just a consumer fix.**

---

## 1. Does it land?

Yes. Two re-adds — `@koc/theme` and `@koc/sidebar` — and nothing in the app
overrides it. Three distinct computed states, in both themes:

| | resting | hover | selected |
|---|---|---|---|
| **light** | transparent · 500 | `accent @ 0.6` | `oklch(0.9364 0.0046 258.33)` · 600 |
| **dark** | transparent · 500 | `accent @ 0.6` | `oklch(0.3528 0.0168 251.8)` · 600 |

The fault you described — hover and selected sharing a fill — is gone. A passing
cursor no longer reads as a selection.

Colour is genuinely transitioned now, confirmed from the computed property list
rather than from the source:

```
transition-property: width, height, padding, background-color, color
transition-duration: 0.12s
transition-timing-function: cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.2, 1)
```

Blue survives exactly where you said it should: the logo tile, the focus ring,
primary buttons. The sidebar reads as chrome now instead of competing with the
content, which was the complaint.

## 2. Does `--accent` moving break anything outside the sidebar?

**Yes, one thing, and the general case is worth your attention more than the
specific fix.**

`--accent` moving to `neutral[100]` made three semantic tokens byte-identical:

```
--accent:    oklch(0.9364 0.0046 258.33)
--secondary: oklch(0.9364 0.0046 258.33)
--muted:     oklch(0.9364 0.0046 258.33)
```

`NilToggle` — the Nil/Reportable control on the report form's safety and delay
questions — used `bg-secondary` for the **selected** half and `hover:bg-accent`
for the other. Those were different colours before this release. They are the
same colour now, so hovering the unselected half rendered it **pixel-identical to
the selected half**:

```
{ text: "None",       pressed: "true",  bg: oklch(0.9364 0.0046 258.33) }
{ text: "Reportable", pressed: "false", bg: oklch(0.9364 0.0046 258.33), hovered: true }
hoverEqualsSelected: true
```

That is the same fault this release set out to fix in the sidebar, reproduced in
a form control as a side effect of the fix.

**Fixed here** the way the sidebar fixes it — hover at 60% of the fill, not the
whole fill. `hoverEqualsSelected` is now `false`. I took the sidebar's own answer
rather than inventing one, so the two controls behave alike.

**The part that is yours, not mine.** A consumer distinguishing two states by
using two different semantic tokens has no way to know whether the system intends
them to stay distinct. Right now `accent`, `secondary` and `muted` are one value,
and nothing says whether that is deliberate convergence or incidental. Two
options, and I have no strong view:

- **Say they may coincide.** Then the guidance is "never distinguish two states by
  using two tokens; vary alpha or weight within one" — which is what the sidebar
  now models and what I have copied.
- **Say they must differ.** Then it wants a check, in the same family as
  `check:parity`: assert that the semantic surface tokens are pairwise distinct
  by some minimum ΔE, so a future colour change cannot silently collapse them.

The second is more work but it is the same shape as the contrast assertions
already in CI, and it would have caught this before the tag.

## 3. Does the weight step read at this density?

Yes — but I would not ship weight *alone*.

`font-medium → font-semibold` is one step, and on the DWOS sidebar it is legible
because the fill changes at the same time. The two signals together are
comfortably enough. I tried reading the nav with the fill mentally removed and
the weight step on its own is marginal at 13px, which is what the sidebar renders
at after the v0.1.2 type scale.

So: no need to widen it as things stand. If you ever remove the fill and lean on
weight alone, widen it then.

## 4. Anything now under-emphasised?

Nothing in the app. I checked every surface of mine that uses `accent` — 21
`hover:bg-accent` sites across the form, the day browser and the task log — and
they were all hover states, none load-bearing for hierarchy. Nothing used
`bg-accent` as a resting or selected state, so there was nothing to lose its
meaning when the hue went.

The task log's row tints (standby amber, transit sky, cargo emerald, downtime
red) are unaffected and now carry *more* of the screen's colour, which reads
better than before — they were previously competing with a blue sidebar.

## Process note

Worth recording because it is the second time it mattered: I synced from the
**tag**, not from `main`. `release:status v0.1.2` reports nine variable changes
against HEAD, and at the time only some of those were in a tag. `sync-registry`
reads through `git show <tag>:`, so unreleased work cannot reach a consuming app
even when it is committed. That guard was written for uncommitted experiments and
has now earned its keep twice against committed-but-unreleased ones.

`release:status` itself was the right thing to build. Separating "changed cssVar,
re-add the theme" from "changed component file, does nothing until re-added" is
exactly the distinction that cost a day when twelve components sat a version
behind, and the new-variable-versus-Tailwind-default check encodes the type-scale
lesson properly.
