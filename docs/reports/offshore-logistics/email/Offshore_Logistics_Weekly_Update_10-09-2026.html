Key highlights
  • OD1 stayed on standby for BOUM-0001 location clearance, now to start jack-down and move to BM-0001. Last week activities are recorded as Nil: the rig was idle for the full seven days.
  • Overstay crew rose from 10 to 11, all of them COSL. KOC and HLB are nil.
  • OD1 personnel onboard rose from 75 to 79. 57 of the 79 hold a seaman or business visa.
  • Ground transport turned sharply inbound: 32 arrivals against 12 dispatches, 44 moves in total against 41. The week before ran 35 out and 6 in, so the direction reversed.
  • The inbound load is the BM-0001 move materials: 18-5/8" and 24" casing and short joints, a casing spool, X-overs, and Fugro positioning tools, from KOC Ahmadi Yard-4 and Yard-5 and HETCO Mina Abdallah. Arrivals peaked on Wed 09 Sep with 9.
  • Fluids handled more than doubled, 1,782 m³ against 742 m³. Fuel delivered to the rigs rose from 200 m³ to 394 m³, of which CA1 transferred 260 m³ on Sun 06 Sep.
  • Vessel trips rose to 13 from 9, per the covering email: CA1 6, CA3 2, CA5 nil, Charlie-3 5. CA1 carried the increase, working OD1 and OPH through the week. See the data queries on the counting basis.
  • OPH stayed off-hired for a third week, and still drew cargo and fluids: CA1 delivered 300 m³ of fresh water and 294 m³ of fuel to OPH across 03 and 07 Sep.
  • Port status reads Normal on all 7 days, where both previous weeks read Not Normal on every day. The port again ran day shift only.
  • Vessel movements remain under MARSEC Level 2.
One trip is one outbound voyage from Shuaiba Port plus its return; a rig to rig transit is not a new trip. A truck move counts one truck unit per dispatch or arrival, and a truck loaded on one day and dispatched the next is one movement, counted on dispatch. Both weeks are counted on that one rule, which is why the previous week reads 41 here against the 46 its own report published.
